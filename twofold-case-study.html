TwoFold

Your trips, two sides to every story.

A shared travel journal for couples and small groups. You planned a trip, TwoFold remembers what actually happened, in everyone's own words.

Problem

Trips are planned together, but remembered apart

Travel apps are good at itineraries and bad at memory. The plan lives in a shared doc, the photos end up in a camera roll, and the real story, the detour that became the best meal or the famous sight that fell flat, is lost within a week. And no two people remember the same day the same way.

The gap between what we hoped for and what actually happened is the most honest part of a trip. Nobody is capturing it.

Solution

A reflection layer on top of the plan

TwoFold is not an itinerary tool and not a photo dump. It should feel like a shared notebook written on a tired evening: warm, minimal and emotionally honest.

Plan and experience, side by side

Every activity card has two halves: "We hoped for" on top, "What happened" below. The contrast between the two is the product.

One moment, many voices

The group plans once, but every member writes their own memory of the same moment. "Best dinner of the trip" and "way too crowded" can both be true.

Unplanned moments count

The crepe stand you stumbled on is often the highlight. Unplanned moments get their own dashed card, never treated as second-class entries.

Ghost cards, not push notifications

Plans that were never logged fade into a quiet dashed card: "You planned this. Did it happen?" No badges, no reminders, no guilt.

Trip Lifecycle

The interface flips with the trip

A trip moves through four states, and the UI is designed to follow that state rather than add separate features: once the trip goes live, "add a plan" prompts give way to "how did it go?" prompts.

Where It Started

The first prototype, built for two

Before it became a shared journal, TwoFold was a single-file web app made for one couple, packaged as an Android APK and synced in real time with Supabase. It answered a simpler question: how do two people plan a trip together? Its warm cream-and-mint look carried straight into the current build. The screens below show a demo trip to Rome that starts in 12 days, with the interface in English.

Pick a Profile, Pick a Trip

No sign-up: the app opens with "Who is travelling?" and two illustrated profiles. Each trip gets a dashboard with a countdown, the weather, the first stop on the plan and a running total of what has been spent.

UX Decisions

Design decisions that matter

Every decision was checked against one question: does this feel like a memory, or like a form?

Never a single flat card

Activities always render in two halves. The plan sits in a sand-colored box; the experience opens underneath in soft mint with a mint edge, so the eye reads expectation first and reality second.

The note is the real voice

A memory is designed to require one sentence, while the verdict and cost stay optional. Typography puts the quote first; pills and prices stay secondary.

"Different", never "conflict"

When friends rate the same moment differently, the group verdict is the average of everyone's voice. A split of WOW, AS HOPED and MEH reads as "as hoped", not as a winner and a loser.

Copy that sounds human

"Add activity" became "What are you hoping for?". An empty day is "This day is still unwritten." Labels invite a story instead of asking for data.

No gamification

No confetti, badges or streaks, and no travel clip-art like passport stamps. Emotional weight comes from contrast, whitespace and the words people write.

Attribution everywhere

Every plan, memory and expense is stored with the person who added it. On the timeline, colored initials make each voice recognizable at a glance without turning it into a social feed.

Final Thoughts

What I'm learning

TwoFold is still being built. Designing it directly in code has shaped the product as much as the wireframes did.

The biggest risk is an empty second half

People come home tired and never write the reflection. The planned answer is a short in-app reflection a couple of days after the trip: one card at a time, one sentence each, under three minutes. It will be an in-app moment, not a push notification.

Scope is a design tool

The MVP is deliberately small: groups, a day-based timeline, plan and experience, and simple expense splitting. No maps, no AI suggestions, no exports. Every "no" made the core idea easier to see.

Multiple voices change the product

With a single memory per activity, TwoFold looked like another itinerary app. Allowing every member their own entry turned it into a shared memory layer, so the data model is built around it: one plan, many experiences, one per member.

What's next

Writing a memory directly from the card, the expenses tab with settle-up, and the post-trip reflection flow, backed by Supabase with row-level security for every group.
