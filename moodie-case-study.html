Problem

Why another mood tracker?

Most mood tracking apps feel clinical and impersonal. Users open them once or twice, then forget about them. The data is useful in theory, but the experience of logging it every day doesn't feel rewarding enough to build a lasting habit.

People don't skip tracking because they don't care about their wellbeing. They skip it because the process feels like a chore.

Design Goals

What I set out to solve

Every design decision was driven by one question: how do we make self-reflection feel natural and even enjoyable?

01
Make tracking feel approachable

Replace clinical inputs with expressive blob characters that users can immediately relate to. A single tap replaces a form.

02
Keep the daily flow under 10 seconds

The core logging action should be fast enough to do on autopilot. Select mood, confirm, done. Journaling is optional, never forced.

03
Show data that tells a story

Mood history, sleep trends, and energy levels are presented as visual narratives, not spreadsheets. Patterns emerge over time without effort.

04
Reward consistency, not perfection

Streaks, achievements, and gentle reminders encourage daily use without inducing guilt when users miss a day.

What I learned

Designing Moodie taught me that wellness products succeed when they respect the user's time and emotional state. Here are the key takeaways.

Character design drives engagement

The blob characters became the emotional core of the app. Users connect with illustrated personalities far more than with abstract icons or sliders. Investing time in character expression paid off in every screen.

Simplicity over feature count

Early versions had too many tracking categories. Narrowing the scope to mood, sleep, and energy created a more focused experience. Users don't need to track everything to gain meaningful insight.

Color as functional language

Each color in the palette maps to a specific mood or feature area. This creates an intuitive visual hierarchy where users can navigate by feeling rather than reading labels.

Habit loops need gentle nudges

The achievement system and configurable reminders were added after realizing that even a delightful experience needs structure to become a daily habit. The balance between motivation and pressure was the hardest part to get right.
